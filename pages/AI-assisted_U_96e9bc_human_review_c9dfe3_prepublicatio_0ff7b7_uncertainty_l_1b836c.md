## Introduction

A weak UFO explanation often becomes “accepted fact” not because the evidence is strong, but because uncertainty quietly disappears during retelling. An AI-generated summary may begin with a cautious match to an [aircraft]({{ 'aircraft/' | relative_url }}), satellite or atmospheric effect, yet end up circulating online as a definitive identification. The problem is rarely outright fabrication. More often, the system treats estimated details as measured facts, smooths over [missing data]({{ 'missing-data/' | relative_url }}), or compresses disagreement into a single confident narrative.

<img src="{{ "/assets/images/AI-assisted_U_96e9bc_human_review_c9dfe3_prepublicatio_0ff7b7_uncertainty_l_1b836c-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Uncertainty labels illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/AI-assisted_U_96e9bc_human_review_c9dfe3_prepublicatio_0ff7b7_uncertainty_l_1b836c-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/AI-assisted_U_96e9bc_human_review_c9dfe3_prepublicatio_0ff7b7_uncertainty_l_1b836c-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
Clear uncertainty labels are therefore not cosmetic wording. They are a core evidence-handling tool in AI-assisted UFO investigation. A public-facing case summary should make it obvious which details were directly measured, which were witness estimates, which were inferred by software, and which remain unknown. NASA’s UAP study stressed that many investigations are weakened by poor [calibration]({{ 'calibration/' | relative_url }}), missing metadata and lack of multiple measurements, while AARO has repeatedly noted that unresolved cases often remain unresolved because available data is insufficient for rigorous analysis. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportAt present, analysis of UAP data is hampered by poor sensor calibration, the lack of multiple me...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Mission_Brief_2025.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The US Defense Department &amp; The UAP MissionMany cases in AARO&#x27;s holdings remain unresolved because of a lack of verifiable data. ## Separating measured facts from witness estimates

The fastest way for a fragile UFO claim to harden is for estimated values to be rewritten as objective measurements. A witness may report that an object was “roughly helicopter height”, “moving at impossible speed”, or “completely silent”. An AI system summarising the report may then convert those impressions into implied facts about altitude, velocity or propulsion.

That shift matters because most UFO conclusions depend on geometry and timing. If the underlying values are uncertain, the explanation built on them may also be unstable.

A reliable case file separates observations into distinct evidence classes:

Evidence typeExampleHow it should be labelledDirect measurementCamera timestamp, radar return, ADS-B aircraft track“Recorded” or “instrument-confirmed”Witness observation“Bright white light moved left to right”“Observed by witness”Witness estimate“About 500 metres away”“Estimated distance”AI inference“Movement pattern resembles aircraft approach path”“Model-generated interpretation”Unknown variableMissing compass direction or duration“Unknown” or “not verified”

Without this separation, readers can mistake interpretation for evidence.

### Speed estimates are especially unreliable

Witnesses are usually poor judges of speed and distance at night. A small nearby object can appear slower than a distant large one. A bright point of light with no depth cues may seem to “jump” across the sky even when moving steadily. If AI systems infer extraordinary acceleration from uncertain range estimates, they can unintentionally exaggerate the anomaly.

A safer public wording is:

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* “Apparent rapid motion reported by witness”
* “Estimated speed cannot be verified from available data” <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.rev.com/transcripts/nasa-holds-first-public-meeting-on-ufos-transcript" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rev.com">[rev.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rev.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA Holds First Public Meeting on UFOs TranscriptThe existing data available from eyewitness reports are often muddled and cannot provid...</span></span></span>
* “No calibrated range measurement available”

</div>

That language keeps the claim attached to the evidence quality.

### Silence is often overinterpreted

“Silent object” reports frequently become stronger in online retellings than the [original]({{ 'original-evidence/' | relative_url }}) testimony supports. Yet many ordinary explanations can appear silent depending on distance, wind direction, indoor observation, glazing, traffic noise or delayed sound arrival.

A strong uncertainty label distinguishes between:

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* “Witness did not report hearing sound”
* “No sound audible from observer location”
* “Object confirmed acoustically silent”

</div>

Only the final statement implies an actual measured absence of sound.

### Time precision should never be implied

Many UFO explanations depend heavily on exact timing. A satellite pass, aircraft movement or re-entry event may appear convincing only if the reported time is accurate to the minute. But witnesses often reconstruct timing after the event.

Public summaries should therefore explicitly state timing quality:

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* Exact recorded time
* Device timestamp
* Approximate recalled time
* Reconstructed estimate
* Time unknown

</div>

NASA’s independent UAP report repeatedly emphasised the importance of calibrated observations and metadata because reliable conclusions depend on well-characterised evidence rather than isolated impressions. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportAt present, analysis of UAP data is hampered by poor sensor calibration, the lack of multiple me...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportAt present, analysis of UAP data is hampered by poor sensor calibration, the lack of multiple me...</span></span></span>

## Writing uncertainty ranges readers can understand

Many technical investigations fail at public communication because uncertainty is expressed in specialist language that ordinary readers cannot interpret consistently. UFO reporting has the opposite problem: uncertainty is often removed entirely in favour of dramatic certainty.

A better approach is calibrated plain-English uncertainty.

Climate science offers a useful model here. The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change developed structured “[confidence]({{ 'confidence/' | relative_url }})” and “likelihood” wording specifically to stop readers overinterpreting uncertain findings. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ipcc.ch/site/assets/uploads/2017/08/AR5_Uncertainty_Guidance_Note.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ipcc.ch">[IPCC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ipcc.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Uncertainties Guidance Note</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Uncertainties Guidance Note - IPCC AR5by MD Mastrandrea · 2010 · Cited by 1234 — These notes define a common approach and calibrated...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10584-011-0178-6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: link.springer.com">[Springer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">link.springer.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The IPCC AR5 guidance note on consistent treatment of...by MD Mastrandrea · 2011 · Cited by 464 — Such calibrated language aims...</span></span></span>

A UFO investigation does not need full scientific probability language, but it can borrow the same principle: use consistent wording tied to evidence quality.

### Useful uncertainty labels for UFO case summaries

LabelMeaningConfirmedSupported by direct measurement or multiple independent sourcesStrongly supportedBest fit for available evidence but not fully verifiedPlausibleConsistent with known evidence but not uniquely supportedWeakly supportedPossible explanation with major unresolved gapsUnresolvedAvailable data insufficient for conclusionContradictedConflicts with verified evidence

The key is consistency. If “likely aircraft” means one thing in one report and another elsewhere, the labels lose value.

### Avoiding misleading numerical confidence

Some AI systems generate percentages such as “87% likely drone”. Those figures can look scientific even when they are based on incomplete or subjective inputs. Unless the system is genuinely calibrated against known historical outcomes, numerical certainty may mislead more than it informs.

This is especially important because research on uncertainty communication shows that people often interpret precise numbers as evidence of objective measurement even when they reflect judgement calls. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10584-022-03382-3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: link.springer.com">[Springer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">link.springer.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">levels and likelihood terms in IPCC reportsby A Kause · 2022 · Cited by 49 — This IPCC guidance note discusses how to use confidence leve...</span></span></span>

A more honest phrasing is usually qualitative:

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* “Most consistent with commercial aircraft”
* “Tentatively matched to Starlink pass”
* “Insufficient evidence to determine object type”

</div>

These statements communicate direction without pretending to impossible precision.

### Readers need to know what could change the conclusion

One of the most useful uncertainty practices is explaining which missing evidence matters most.

For example:

> “The balloon explanation remains plausible, but no wind data from the reported altitude range is available.”

Or:

> “The aircraft match depends on the witness time estimate being accurate within approximately two minutes.”

This helps readers understand that the uncertainty is specific and testable rather than vague evasiveness.

## Avoiding false certainty in public AI summaries

AI systems naturally produce fluent narratives. That fluency creates a serious risk in UFO investigation because readers often mistake coherence for proof.

A weak explanation can harden through several stages:

1. Witness estimate becomes structured data
2. AI correlates it with a possible explanation
3. Summary language removes caveats
4. Social [reposts]({{ 'reposts/' | relative_url }}) shorten the wording further
5. Tentative match becomes “debunked” or “confirmed”

Once that process begins, later corrections rarely travel as widely as the original claim.

<img src="{{ "/assets/images/AI-assisted_U_96e9bc_human_review_c9dfe3_prepublicatio_0ff7b7_uncertainty_l_1b836c-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Uncertainty labels illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/AI-assisted_U_96e9bc_human_review_c9dfe3_prepublicatio_0ff7b7_uncertainty_l_1b836c-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/AI-assisted_U_96e9bc_human_review_c9dfe3_prepublicatio_0ff7b7_uncertainty_l_1b836c-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
### Compression removes nuance first

A long technical case file may correctly state:

> “Possible aircraft correlation based on estimated timing and directional alignment, though altitude and sound characteristics remain unverified.”

A short social summary may become:

> “AI identified the UFO as an aircraft.”

The uncertainty vanished not because anyone deliberately lied, but because compressed retellings preferentially remove qualifiers.

Public-facing AI outputs should therefore place uncertainty directly beside the claim, not buried later in the document.

Good format:

* “Plausible aircraft match (timing uncertain)”
* “Possible satellite flare (direction estimate low confidence)”

Bad format:

* “Aircraft identified”
* “Likely satellite”

### AI systems should preserve disagreement, not flatten it

Some UFO reports genuinely contain conflicting evidence. One witness may describe hovering while another describes linear motion. Video may suggest one interpretation while [astronomy]({{ 'astronomy/' | relative_url }}) data suggests another.

A strong review process allows disagreement to remain visible instead of forcing a single clean conclusion.

For example:

Evidence elementSupports aircraftConflicts with aircraftFlight path correlationYes—Reported silent hovering—YesFlashing navigation patternYes—Abrupt vertical motion claim—Unverified

This structure prevents the AI summary from pretending the evidence is internally consistent when it is not.

That distinction matters. “Unresolved” does not mean alien craft, secret technology or debunked hoax. It simply means the evidence cannot support a stable conclusion.

Public AI summaries should therefore resist the pressure to always provide closure. In some cases, the most accurate statement is:

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* data incomplete
* metadata missing
* timing uncertain
* explanation inconclusive

</div>

That restraint protects the credibility of the wider investigation process.

## What a responsible UFO conclusion should openly admit

A well-written AI-assisted UFO conclusion should leave readers with a clear understanding not only of what the investigation found, but also of what it could not establish.

At minimum, a responsible conclusion should explicitly state:

* whether timing was measured or estimated
* whether direction and [elevation]({{ 'elevation/' | relative_url }}) were verified

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* whether imagery retained original metadata
* whether the explanation depends on uncertain assumptions
* whether alternative explanations remain plausible
* whether the available evidence can support a firm conclusion at all

</div>
