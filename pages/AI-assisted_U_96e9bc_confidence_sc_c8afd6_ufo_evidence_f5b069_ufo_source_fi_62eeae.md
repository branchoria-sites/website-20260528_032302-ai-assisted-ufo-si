## Introduction

A viral UFO clip can attract millions of views while becoming nearly useless for serious investigation. The problem is not always deception. More often, the original recording has been stripped of the very information investigators need to test ordinary explanations properly. Once a video has been reposted through social media platforms, screen-recorded, cropped, edited, compressed, or uploaded without its source file, critical [metadata]({{ 'metadata/' | relative_url }}) can disappear. That [missing data]({{ 'missing-data/' | relative_url }}) may include the exact recording time, device details, GPS coordinates, frame structure, compression history, and signs of editing.

<img src="{{ "/assets/images/AI-assisted_U_96e9bc_confidence_sc_c8afd6_ufo_evidence_f5b069_ufo_source_fi_62eeae-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Source Files illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/AI-assisted_U_96e9bc_confidence_sc_c8afd6_ufo_evidence_f5b069_ufo_source_fi_62eeae-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/AI-assisted_U_96e9bc_confidence_sc_c8afd6_ufo_evidence_f5b069_ufo_source_fi_62eeae-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
In AI-assisted UFO sighting investigation, this matters because modern analysis depends less on spectacle and more on verification. A dramatic light in the sky cannot be compared properly against aircraft tracks, satellite passes, astronomical objects, weather conditions, or drone activity unless the [original evidence]({{ 'original-evidence/' | relative_url }}) survives intact. NASA’s independent UAP study repeatedly stressed that poor metadata and inconsistent sensor information are major barriers to meaningful analysis. ## What original media preserves that reposts destroy

The original source file from a phone, camera, drone, CCTV system, or aircraft recorder often contains layers of information invisible to viewers. Investigators are not only examining what appears on screen. They are also examining how, when, and where the file was created.

A genuine source file may preserve:

* Exact creation timestamp
* GPS coordinates

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* Device make and model
* Frame rate and resolution
* Lens settings and zoom level
* Orientation data
* Encoding structure
* Audio synchronisation
* Compression history
* Embedded thumbnails and editing traces

</div>

This information can become more valuable than the visible object itself. A timestamp accurate to the second may allow investigators to compare the sighting against ADS-B aircraft transponder data, satellite databases, Starlink passes, ISS visibility, rocket launches, meteor reports, or weather radar. Without that timestamp, multiple ordinary explanations can remain impossible to confirm or rule out.

Exif metadata, widely used in digital photographs and videos, is especially important in digital forensics because it preserves timestamps, device identifiers, and sometimes geolocation data. This creates a major problem for UFO investigation workflows. AI systems can only compare a sighting against external datasets if the sighting has reliable anchors in time and place. A reposted clip with no original metadata forces investigators into approximation rather than verification.

The distinction becomes obvious in practical cases. A glowing object filmed above a city at “around 10 pm last summer” is difficult to analyse rigorously. The same object, tied to an exact UTC timestamp and phone orientation, may quickly match a satellite flare, an aircraft approach pattern, or a re-entering rocket stage.

## How compression and screen recording alter evidence

Many viral UFO clips are no longer direct recordings. They are copies of copies.

A common chain looks like this:

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. Original phone recording
2. Upload to TikTok, Instagram, X, Facebook, or YouTube
3. Download by another user
4. Re-upload with filters, captions, or zoom
5. Screen-recorded reaction video
6. Cropped repost shared across multiple platforms

</div>

By the time investigators encounter the clip, the visible object may still exist, but much of the forensic value has disappeared.

### Compression changes motion and light behaviour

Social media platforms routinely compress uploaded media to reduce bandwidth and storage costs. Compression algorithms remove visual information that platforms consider unnecessary for ordinary viewing. Unfortunately, these discarded details can matter enormously in UFO analysis.

Compression can:

* Smear small light sources
* Introduce artificial flickering
* Create blocky motion artefacts
* Exaggerate glowing edges
* Blur stars or [reference points]({{ 'reference-points/' | relative_url }})

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* Distort colour transitions
* Alter exposure behaviour
* Remove subtle background detail

</div>

A distant aircraft light can appear to “pulse” unnaturally after aggressive compression. Heat haze or atmospheric turbulence may look like shape-shifting motion once encoded at lower quality. Tiny camera movements can become exaggerated through stabilisation algorithms.

Research into forensic video analysis notes that recompression and social-media transfer can significantly affect video integrity and reduce analytical reliability.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://ijsrcseit.com/CSEIT2390373" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ijsrcseit.com">[IJSRCSEIT]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ijsrcseit.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Significance of Metadata and Video Compression for...30 Jun 2023 — We first propose the acquisition extraction analysis in...</span></span></span> Academia This is especially dangerous for AI-assisted systems because machine-learning tools may confidently classify artefacts introduced by compress<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.academia.edu/103752968/The_Significance_of_Metadata_and_Video_Compression_for_Investigating_Video_Files_on_Social_Media_Forensic" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[academia.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International Journal of Scientific Research in...Read more...</span></span></span> ion rather than properties of the original object.

### Screen recordings erase the original structure

Screen recordings are even more destructive from an investigative perspective.

When someone records a video playing on another device, the new file no longer contains the original recording structure. Instead, it becomes a recording of a display surface. Important information disappears entirely:

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Original encoding signatures
* Native frame timing
* Sensor metadata<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.wired.com/story/metadata-shows-the-dojs-raw-jeffrey-epstein-prison-video-was-likely-modified" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wired.com">[wired.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wired.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Metadata Shows the FBI&#x27;s &#x27;Raw&#x27; Jeffrey Epstein Prison Video Was Likely Modified</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of Justice recently released nearly 11 hours of surveillance footage from Jeffrey Epstein&#x27;s prison area, labeled as &quot;raw&quot; vide...</span></span></span>
* Original audio synchronisation
* Camera-specific compression patterns
* Device-origin information

</div>

Investigators also lose the ability to determine whether playback speed changed during reposting. A slowed-down aircraft beacon pattern or edited playback sequence can create false impressions of unusual motion.

Digital forensics specialists often analyse encoding histories and metadata structures to detect editing or tampering. Once a clip becomes a screen recording, much of that forensic trail is gone.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://eclipseforensics.com/spotting-the-fakes-understanding-video-authentication-techniques/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eclipseforensics.com">[Eclipse Forensics]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eclipseforensics.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">spotting the fakes understanding video authentication techniques</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eclipse ForensicsSpotting the Fakes: Understanding Video Authentication...24 Dec 2025 — Through forensic video analysis, specialists exa...</span></span></span>

## Why chain of custody matters in UFO investigations

“Chain of custody” sounds like courtroom language, but the concept is highly relevant to UFO evidence grading.

In practice, chain of custody means documenting how a file moved from its original source to investigators without undocumented alteration. The longer and less traceable that path becomes, the harder it is to trust the evidence.

For a UFO sighting, useful chain-of-custody questions include:

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Who recorded the footage?
* Is the original file still available?<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.axon.com/resources/digital-evidence-management-guide" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: axon.com">[axon.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">axon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Digital Evidence Management: The Definitive GuideObtaining the original file ensures you review the truest source of information availabl...</span></span></span>
* Was it transferred directly from the device?
* Which apps processed it?
* Was the video edited before upload?
* Were filters or stabilisation tools applied?
* Are there multiple versions online?
* Which upload appeared first?

</div>

An AI model may identify similarities to drones, aircraft lights, balloons, or satellites based on shape and motion patterns. Yet if the timestamp is uncertain by even an hour, the system may be comparing the clip against the wrong aircraft traffic or satellite positions entirely.

## The practical investigative difference between “interesting” and “usable”

In public discussion, UFO footage is often judged emotionally. Investigators use a different standard: can the evidence support reconstruction?

A reusable source file allows investigators to:

* Rebuild the viewing [geometry]({{ 'geometry/' | relative_url }})
* Estimate object direction and elevation
* Compare against local flight traffic
* Match satellite trajectories
* Analyse environmental conditions
* Examine frame-level motion
* Detect editing traces
* Cross-check witness timing
* Estimate distance uncertainty
* Test competing explanations systematically

A viral repost usually cannot support most of those steps.

That difference explains why experienced investigators sometimes dismiss visually impressive clips while taking more interest in mundane-looking recordings with preserved metadata and corroborating context. A tiny distant light recorded with precise timing and location can often produce stronger conclusions than a spectacular-looking repost with no provenance.

In AI-assisted UFO investigation, the original file is not merely the “best version” of the evidence. It is often the only version capable of supporting meaningful verification at all.
