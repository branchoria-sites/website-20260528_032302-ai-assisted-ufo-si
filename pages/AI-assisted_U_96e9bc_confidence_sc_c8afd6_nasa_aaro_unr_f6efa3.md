## Introduction

Official UFO and UAP investigations by NASA and the US Department of Defense’s All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) point to a less dramatic but more useful conclusion than many public debates suggest: a significant number of cases remain unresolved because the evidence is [incomplete]({{ 'incomplete-data/' | relative_url }}), low quality, fragmented, or impossible to verify with [confidence]({{ 'confidence/' | relative_url }}). That does not automatically make those cases extraordinary. In many instances, investigators cannot prove either a mundane explanation or an anomalous one. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportAt present, analysis of UAP data is hampered by poor sensor calibration, the lack of multiple me...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP ImageryThe United States European Command submitted a report of an unidentified anomalous phenomenon to the All-domain Anomaly Re...</span></span></span>

This distinction matters for AI-assisted UFO sighting investigation. A good investigative workflow should not force every report into “explained” or “alien”. ## Why official UFO cases stay unresolved

One of the clearest findings from recent official UAP work is that unresolved cases frequently share the same structural weaknesses:

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* short clips with no wider context
* missing sensor metadata <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[science.nasa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportAt present, analysis of UAP data is hampered by poor sensor calibration, the lack of multiple me...</span></span></span>
* uncertain timestamps
* no independent corroboration
* absent range or altitude information
* poor calibration data <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[science.nasa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportAt present, analysis of UAP data is hampered by poor sensor calibration, the lack of multiple me...</span></span></span>
* only one observation angle
* compressed or degraded imagery

</div>

NASA’s independent UAP study explicitly stated that analysis is hindered by “poor sensor calibration”, lack of multiple measurements, limited metadata, and insufficient baseline information. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportAt present, analysis of UAP data is hampered by poor sensor calibration, the lack of multiple me...</span></span></span>

That creates a practical problem for both human investigators and AI systems. A blurry infrared object may look unusual, but without reliable information about:

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* camera zoom level
* aircraft motion
* viewing angle
* atmospheric conditions
* sensor mode
* frame timing

</div>

it may be impossible to estimate speed, size, distance, or manoeuvrability accurately.

This is one reason official investigations often avoid dramatic conclusions. AARO repeatedly distinguishes between:

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* resolved cases
* not anomalous cases <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://defensescoop.com/2024/11/14/uap-aaro-chief-unveils-pentagon-annual-caseload-analysis-new-efforts/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defensescoop.com">[defensescoop.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defensescoop.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">uap aaro chief unveils pentagon annual caseload analysis new efforts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The truly anomalous&#x27;: New AARO chief unveils Pentagon&#x27;s...14 Nov 2024 — “AARO has successfully resolved hundreds of cases in its holdin...</span></span></span>

* unresolved reports

</div>

Those categories are important because they acknowledge uncertainty instead of masking it. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO HomeUnidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP) means (A) airborne objects that are not immediately identifiable; (B) transmedium objects...</span></span></span>

For AI-assisted investigation systems, this becomes a design lesson: confidence scoring should include confidence in the underlying evidence itself, not just confidence in a proposed explanation.

## NASA’s core lesson: better data matters more than better speculation

NASA’s public position on UAP has been notably restrained. The agency’s study team repeatedly argued that the biggest obstacle is not lack of imagination, but lack of reliable observational data. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.space.com/nasa-ufo-study-group-better-data-needed" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: space.com">[Space]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">space.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nasa ufo study group better data needed</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs will remain mysterious without better data, NASA...31 May 2023 — NASA&#x27;s UAP study team stressed that the biggest roadblock sta...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2023</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportAt present, analysis of UAP data is hampered by poor sensor calibration, the lack of multiple me...</span></span></span>

The agency emphasised several principles directly relevant to UFO case-file analysis:

### Metadata is often more important than imagery

A dramatic-looking image without timing, [geolocation]({{ 'geolocation/' | relative_url }}), orientation, or sensor calibration can be nearly useless analytically. A less striking image with complete metadata may be far more valuable.

For example, an AI system trying to evaluate a sighting needs:

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* precise observer location
* exact time
* camera field of view
* lens characteristics
* movement of the recording platform
* weather conditions
* astronomical context

</div>

Without these, apparent motion can easily be misinterpreted.

NASA’s report repeatedly stressed that scientific conclusions require curated and standardised data rather than isolated viral clips. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportAt present, analysis of UAP data is hampered by poor sensor calibration, the lack of multiple me...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.wired.com/story/nasa-ufos-aliens-report-2023" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wired.com">[WIRED]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wired.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The agency stressed the need to shift the conversation from sensationalism to science and eliminate the stigma associated with reporting...</span></span></span>

### Single-sensor sightings are weak evidence

One recurring issue in unresolved cases is dependence on a lone sensor stream. A thermal camera may detect an object, but without radar, visual confirmation, telemetry, or independent observers, interpretation becomes difficult.

An AI-assisted investigation platform should therefore weight:

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* multi-sensor agreement
* independent witness consistency
* environmental correlation
* external verification sources

</div>

more heavily than raw visual novelty.

### Stigma can reduce reporting quality

### Balloon resolutions are especially instructive

Several AARO cases from Europe in 2022 were eventually resolved as balloons after further analysis. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP ImageryThe United States European Command submitted a report of an unidentified anomalous phenomenon to the All-domain Anomaly Re...</span></span></span>

Balloons create recurring investigative problems because:

* range is difficult to judge visually
* infrared imagery can distort apparent shape
* [wind drift]({{ 'wind-drift/' | relative_url }}) may appear unusual
* lack of background reference points confuses speed estimation
* zoom compression alters perceived motion

An AI system trained only on public “mystery footage” could incorrectly classify these clips as highly anomalous if it lacked:

* local wind data
* altitude modelling
* camera motion correction
* known balloon signatures

This is a strong argument for environmental correlation workflows in UFO case analysis.

Infrared footage can make ordinary objects appear highly unusual:

* flapping may disappear at low frame rates
* thermal bloom can distort shape
* depth cues vanish
* multiple objects can merge visually

To a viewer unfamiliar with sensor artefacts, such footage may appear to show structured craft or coordinated manoeuvres.

For AI-assisted investigation, this demonstrates why training data must include:

* false positives
* ordinary aerial clutter
* sensor artefacts
* known atmospheric distortions

rather than only famous UFO clips.

## What unresolved really means

One of the biggest public misunderstandings is treating “unresolved” as equivalent to “confirmed extraordinary”.

Official investigations do not use the term that way.

AARO’s unresolved cases often mean: <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://usaherald.com/us-government-pentagon-releases-new-ufo-files-sparking-fresh-questions-as-declassified-uap-evidence-surges/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: usaherald.com">[usaherald.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">usaherald.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">US Government: Pentagon Releases New UFO Files...16 hours ago — The Pentagon&#x27;s All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office, known as AARO, main...</span></span></span>

* there is insufficient information for identification
* available footage confirms an object exists but not what it is
* analysts cannot responsibly eliminate multiple possibilities
* key contextual data are missing
* the evidence does not meet closure standards

In some unresolved cases, AARO states only that a physical object appears present, while declining to identify its nature. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Records/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Records</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">/Information Papers13 Feb 2026 — The official website for the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)... That is a much narrower claim than many online discussions imply.

### Unresolved can mean “low-information”

A twenty-second infrared clip may simply not contain enough information to estimate:

* acceleration
* true speed
* altitude
* dimensions
* propulsion
* trajectory

Without those variables, analysts cannot reliably distinguish between:

* distant aircraft
* balloons
* drones
* birds
* optical effects
* genuinely unusual objects

This is why AI confidence scoring should avoid [false precision]({{ 'false-precision/' | relative_url }}). A system claiming “94% anomalous” from sparse evidence would be behaving less cautiously than official investigators.

## Lessons for AI confidence scoring

The strongest lesson from NASA and AARO is methodological rather than sensational.

An AI-assisted UFO investigation platform should behave less like an oracle and more like a structured evidence triage system.

### Confidence should be conditional

Instead of:

* “This is probably extraterrestrial”

a responsible system should say:

* “Available evidence weakly matches balloon behaviour”
* “Aircraft explanation cannot be ruled out”
* “Insufficient data for reliable classification”

Although unresolved does not mean extraordinary, unresolved cases are still valuable.

* sensor coverage

* cross-system integration
* observational reliability
